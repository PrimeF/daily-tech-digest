## Top Stories

US Thanksgiving made this a quiet window. No frontier lab released a model, and the cloud providers announced little. The three stories below are the ones with real consequences for architects.

### 1. Update: Nvidia pauses server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia

**What's new (the BIS remote-access rule was first covered on 17 Nov).** Nvidia has told Dell, Super Micro and other server builders to stop shipping systems with its AI chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia "for an unspecified period". The Wall Street Journal says Nvidia is checking whether regional partners meet US export requirements. The Financial Times, citing three people, says Dell and Super Micro have told buyers their orders cannot ship ([TechCrunch on the WSJ report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/26/nvidia-hits-pause-on-chip-shipments-to-southeast-asia/); [The Decoder on the FT report](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-to-malaysia-singapore-thailand-and-indonesia-after-tighter-us-export-controls/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-halts-server-shipments-southeast-asia)). The rule took effect on 19 Nov and also covers Brazil, which the reports do not mention. Operators that already rent out compute have 90 days to get licences. The Decoder, citing CNBC, says dealers are listing thousands of B300 GPUs that were meant for data centres with Chinese rental customers. The Institute for Progress estimates that remote data centres like these supply about 24% of China's AI compute (via TechCrunch).

**Knock-on effect on Chinese labs.** A Reuters exclusive, read here through a summary by Unite.ai (an AI-written piece its editors reviewed), says Z.ai's roughly 3-trillion-parameter GLM-5.5 has slipped from November to Q1 2027 or later. The reasons it gives are the new compute rule and the labs' anti-distillation safeguards. The same report says Moonshot now distils from Google and Meta models because OpenAI and Anthropic outputs have become harder to get, and that Kimi K4 could slip to mid-2027 ([Unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/moonshot-ai-begins-distilling-from-google-and-meta-models-amid-delays/)). Reuters was not read directly (see Access Notes).

**Why it matters.** Compliance has moved from Commerce's licence office to the companies that ship the servers, because the rule depends on what an exporter knows about where a server ends up ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/331670/20261127/nvidia-reportedly-tells-dell-super-micro-stop-server-shipments-southeast-asia.htm)). If you plan GPU capacity in Southeast Asia, whether through neoclouds, colocation or a sovereign deployment, treat delivery dates as uncertain until licensing guidance arrives. Also check whether your providers' tenant screening is strong enough to keep their licences. On HN, the main reaction was doubt that the pause will stop diversion: a top comment expects shipments to simply reroute to other regions ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50653045), via last30days).

### 2. OpenAI fixes a GPT-6.2 billing mistake, and paid plans now run out faster

**What happened.** On 26 Nov Codex lead Thibault Sottiaux wrote that a "billing mistake at launch of GPT-6.2 … incorrectly discounted GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol usage for most users". After the fix, users reach their limits 40% faster on GPT-6.2 Astra and 25% faster on Sol than they did the week before ([X post, read via oEmbed](https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2125784147889393806)). The HN thread reached about 480 points and 267 comments (via last30days). A second post the same day, "OpenAI may be in cost control mode" (about 317 points and 325 comments), uses the author's own "Agent Duel" leaderboard data. It finds that Astra 6.2 and Sol 6.2 produce significantly fewer output and reasoning tokens per task than their predecessors and rival models (reasoning-token drop p = 0.00013 for Sol and 0.0064 for Astra; one author's benchmark, not independently confirmed) ([micjm.com](https://micjm.com/blog/openai-is-in-cost-control-mode/)).

**Why it matters.** This comes on top of the merged Chat/Work usage meter covered on 26 Nov. Within a week, the effective capacity of a ChatGPT Work or Codex seat has changed twice without any change in list price. If your teams budget agent work in plan allowances rather than API tokens, re-baseline this week. If you depend on GPT-6.2's quality at a fixed price, measure it yourself: fewer reasoning tokens per task can show up as quality loss on long-horizon agent work even when benchmark scores look flat.

### 3. Update: Google's Workspace MCP mandate meets its first detailed critique

**What's new (Workspace MCP GA and the "agents must use MCP" addendum were covered on 25 Nov).** A Tech Stackups review of Google's server documentation found large gaps (HN about 314 points, 268 comments). The public **Gmail MCP server can search, read, create drafts and manage labels, but it cannot send, reply, forward or edit a draft**. Google's quota page says those tools "require partner access and aren't generally available". Drive cannot move, rename, share or trash files. Docs, Slides and Tasks are close to parity with REST ([Tech Stackups](https://techstackups.com/articles/google-workspace-ai-agents-mcp/)). Other details the article reports from Google's documentation:

- The free Standard tier allows 5,000 MCP requests per day per project per product. A Gmail `search_threads` call costs 10 units, so the limit works out to 500 searches a day for the whole project.
- The paid Scaled tier starts billing on 1 Apr 2027 and charges MCP usage from the first request, while REST keeps large free daily allowances.
- Google can cap retention of "Google Technical Information" at 30 days. The definition includes vector embeddings, memory indexes and organisational graphs built from Workspace data.
- OAuth clients created before 11 Nov that were not marked as agentic in time stay non-agentic permanently.

**Why it matters.** According to the article, Anthropic's Workspace connector advertises sending email and moving Drive files, which suggests partners get capabilities that independent agent builders do not. For an enterprise architect, the 30-day cap is the bigger design constraint: any long-lived RAG index or agent memory built from Workspace data needs a rebuild-or-expire pipeline before 1 Feb 2027. The top HN comment predicted that agents will simply drive a browser to send email instead (via last30days). That is exactly the uncontrolled path the mandate is meant to close.

Vendor-reported: GRPO cut repetition-loop pages from 126 to 29. DFlash2 delivers 20.44 pages/s at concurrency 512 on one H200, against 13.48 without speculation and 2.98 for Parse 2.0. The model uses the OpenMDW 1.1 licence ([Hugging Face blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/building-on-nemotron-35-parse-open-data-fine-tunin)). For document-ingestion pipelines, the recipe shows how verifiable rewards can fix looping and truncation in structured extraction.

### Security & Industry

- **Update on the OpenRouter provider incident (first covered 23 Nov):** an independent investigation traces DeepSeek traffic sold through Novita, StreamLake and Ant Digital to ByteDance's Ark API. Its evidence is matching gateway error strings and tool-call ID formats, upstream request IDs, and which networks download images sent in prompts (Volcano Engine and BytePlus address ranges). It also notes that OpenRouter withdrew Novita's ZDR label some time before 20 Nov and Ant Digital's on 22 Nov ([kendell.dev](https://kendell.dev/blog/arkwrapper/); single independent source, HN about 89 points). If you route through aggregators, the lesson is that ZDR and data-residency labels describe the reseller, not the GPU your tokens actually land on. Make your contracts cover sub-processors, and pin providers explicitly.

### Research

- **How much do coding agents over-edit? (arXiv 2611.34875):** across 7,660 resolved GitHub Copilot CLI trajectories from 18 models, agents' code was on average at least as necessary as developers' code. On SWE-bench Verified, the extra edits were mostly tests (61%) and docs (13%). About 11% of agent code units were still unneeded, and 89% of those changed behaviour. Code necessity did not track resolve rate on SWE-bench Verified and was negatively correlated with it on DeepSWE (Spearman −0.59) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34875)). This supports adding harness-level pruning and patch-necessity checks to agent CI, not only pass/fail.
- **SkillsBench audit (arXiv 2611.33943):** a source-level reanalysis of 7,554 trials found two cases of grader dependence and placeholder skill directories. After multiple-testing correction, no task-level harm from skills was statistically significant, and skill-gating strategies did not beat a shuffled-label control on held-out runs ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33943)). Be careful when gating skills on single-benchmark lift numbers.
- **Skills "enabled but underused" (arXiv 2611.34250):** every model and harness combination tested on EBU-Bench used a medical skill much less accurately on its own than when told to read and apply it, with a mean gap of 43.94 points. For the same model, accuracy varied by up to 38.96 points across harnesses ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34250)).
- **TransHarness (arXiv 2611.33757):** evolves harness interventions from success/failure contrasts using only 300 out-of-distribution trajectories. It reports 76.4% on DeepSWE v1.1 and 95.5% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (author-reported) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33757)).

## Engineering Blogs & Tech Press

- **Databricks, "Rethinking read replicas for the lakebase architecture" (26 Nov):** on Lakebase (Neon-style disaggregated Postgres), a read replica is just another compute attached to the primary's storage. It is initialised from a slim base backup on the pageserver, streams WAL from the Paxos-replicated safekeepers rather than from the primary, and reads pages with GetPage@LSN. Creation takes seconds regardless of database size. Three replicas of a 1 TB database store 1 TB, not 4 TB. Replicas suspend after 5 minutes idle and resume without a WAL replay backlog (vendor-reported). The trade-offs: read-your-writes traffic stays on the primary, caches start cold, and every compute shares one storage layer, a shared blast radius the post does not discuss. HA is deferred to a later post ([Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/rethinking-read-replicas-lakebase-architecture)). Per-workload replicas, including read-only replicas that agents spin up for themselves, become cheap enough to use routinely.
- **Hugging Face, "Swarms, Flocks, Gangs", part 1 (26 Nov):** a reproducible multi-agent RL setup in which four agents share one Qwen3-0.6B policy, each with a private context. They communicate through `message`/`read_messages` tools and train on a shared team reward discounted by tokens used (about five hours on two H200s, roughly $50). The series frames its key question as whether communication helps at a matched token budget or just buys more attempts. Part 1 tests only the precondition, a task that cannot be solved without sharing information ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/training-swarms)).
